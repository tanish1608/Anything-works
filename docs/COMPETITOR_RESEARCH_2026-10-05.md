# Everything Works AI — US competitor diligence

Updated October 5, 2026. Public-source desk research; no sales demos, customer interviews, or authenticated product testing. This revision evaluates the near-term product. The broader agentic construction operating system is the long-term vision, not the product we need to match against every incumbent feature today.

**Geographic scope:** US-headquartered operating businesses from the original comparison. Fieldwire remains because it is based in San Francisco, although its parent Hilti is based outside the US. Companies headquartered outside the US have been removed from the table, profiles, sources and recommendations. Headquarters is a report filter, not proof that international vendors cannot sell to US customers. This is not an exhaustive map of vendors serving the US.

**The product being evaluated:** Make ordinary field updates useful for coordinating work: collect photos and short updates, with voice capture in the proposed workflow; connect them to the right room, trade and planned work; surface missing evidence or unresolved issues; let the responsible person correct them and the PM approve; show what is ready for inspection or the next trade. The floor plan/3D view supplies location context. AI assists organization and review. The first outcome is earlier correction and clearer handoffs.

That description is the intended initial workflow, not a claim that every part is already production-ready. The repository's pitch describes evidence capture, location-linked issues and approval foundations alongside unfinished coordination capabilities. Its prototype and demonstration do not yet establish real-site accuracy, adoption or savings. [Project pitch](PITCH.md).

**Revised assessment:** This is a more credible initial product than attempting to replace the entire construction software stack. In this retained set, OpenSpace Field and Fieldwire are the most immediate workflow benchmarks; Procore is the incumbent platform and potential integration destination; Trunk Tools has strongest overlap when we expand into document intelligence. There is room to test a focused product, but neither this geographic filter nor a simpler interface establishes an uncontested market.

**How to read the numbers:** All monetary figures below are USD. Funding is money raised; valuation is an equity-value estimate or transaction reference; public market capitalization changes daily; acquisition value is historical transaction consideration. LinkedIn headcount bands are company-profile disclosures, not audited payroll. Product descriptions are vendor claims unless specified otherwise. The financing figures retain the dates and qualifications from the original research.

## What the products actually offer

### 1. Trunk Tools: construction knowledge and AI agents

Trunk Tools offers cited project answers over text messages, submittal analysis, register creation, drawing-revision comparison, RFI drafting, bid analysis and change-event analysis. It markets a construction knowledge graph and in-person field onboarding. Its site claims deployment on 1,000 US projects representing $50B in construction volume; neither figure is revenue. Founder/CEO is Dr. Sarah Buchner. [Current product site](https://trunktools.com/).

Its 2025 Series B was led by Insight Partners with Redpoint, Innovation Endeavors and others. Forge's valuation is expressly an estimate based on company-submitted Certificates of Incorporation; use it with that qualifier. No current public list price was verified.

**Relevance to our initial product:** Adjacent when our focus is field evidence, corrections and accepted handoffs. More direct if we make document question-answering, RFI drafting or submittal review the main product. Its breadth should inform the long-term roadmap, rather than become a feature checklist for the first release.

### 2. OpenSpace Field: visual evidence becomes a field action

OpenSpace has expanded beyond reality capture. Field advertises photo-and-voice issue creation, automated assignees/dates/tags, and suggested placement on a drawing. Autolocation uses a smartphone plus a previous 360° capture. It supports two-way synchronization of Procore observations/punch items and Autodesk issues. Field is included in Core and Enterprise subscriptions. The product page explicitly keeps Procore/Autodesk as the system of record. [Field product description](https://www.openspace.ai/products/field/).

**Relevance to our initial product:** Very direct. It already connects field media to located issues and existing project records. Our test should compare how easily a team goes from a reported problem to an approved room handoff, including missing prerequisites. The previous 360° capture requirement applies to its autolocation feature; do not claim every OpenSpace workflow requires that setup. Whether our complete readiness flow is materially simpler remains untested.

### 3. Fieldwire: established field execution and coordination

Fieldwire links plans, tasks, photos and checklists; higher tiers include forms, integrations, BIM viewing, RFIs, submittals, change orders and budgets. Its US pricing page lists Basic free, Pro $39, Business $64 and Business Plus $89 per user/month, paid annually. Limits and included workflows differ. [Features and pricing](https://www.fieldwire.com/pricing/).

Hilti's ownership matters because the competitor has an established construction distribution channel. The company LinkedIn profile claims use on 4M+ projects; this is cumulative usage, not a paying-customer count.

**Relevance to our initial product:** Direct. This is the benchmark for practical mobile coordination and the existing workflow a buyer may already understand. We should demonstrate fewer manual steps between field submission, correction and approval. Its published pricing also means affordability needs a real comparison, not an assumption that every incumbent is expensive.

### 4. Procore: platform, distribution and embedded agents

Procore now advertises agents for daily logs from photos/voice/video, document search, field observations, RFIs, drawing analysis, submittal review and schedule analysis, with review and approval in the workflow. [Agent catalog](https://www.procore.com/ai/agents). Procore Connect is a capability within this company, not a separate startup.

The Q2 2026 release reports 2,871 organic customers above $100,000 ARR and an expanded AI experience incorporating Datagrid. SEC filings state it bought Datagrid on January 16, 2026 for $168M cash purchase consideration. [SEC filing, acquisition note](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1611052/000162828026051460/pcor-20260630.htm). Its DroneDeploy acquisition closed September 9, 2026. [Completion announcement](https://www.procore.com/blog/procore-acquires-dronedeploy-to-bring-visual-intelligence-to-every-jobsite).

Pricing is quote-based by products and annual construction volume, with unlimited users. Free collaborator access is therefore not unique to a startup. [Pricing model](https://www.procore.com/pricing).

**Relevance to our initial product:** Broad overlap, but we do not need to replace the platform to test our focused workflow. A GC could keep its official project records there while using our product for evidence collection and readiness review. We would still need to earn the additional subscription and integration effort. Describe the operating-system ambition as future expansion; compare the initial product on actual user effort and outcomes.

## Revised implications for Everything Works AI

The assessment changes because the near-term scope is narrower. Removing overseas headquarters alone does not improve the product's economics or establish uniqueness.

| Remaining competitor | Main overlap with today's intended workflow | What we need to demonstrate |
|---|---|---|
| OpenSpace Field | Photo/voice capture, location-linked issues, integration with project records | Easier setup and fewer manual steps to an accepted handoff for our selected customers |
| Fieldwire | Plans, tasks, field evidence, coordination and reporting | A substantial improvement over configuring and operating its existing tasks and forms |
| Procore | Official project workflows, observations, quality records and increasingly AI-assisted administration | A focused improvement worth adopting alongside the customer's existing system |
| Trunk Tools | Construction document context and AI-assisted records | Keep the initial comparison on field execution; prove any document intelligence we add |

These comparisons do not assert that competitors lack readiness rules or approvals. Public marketing pages are insufficient to establish an absent capability.

### What I would pitch now

> Everything Works AI helps construction teams turn field updates into clear actions and verified handoffs, so they can catch unfinished work before inspections or the next trade arrives.

A concrete demo should show a foreman submitting an update for Unit 304, the system surfacing an outstanding prerequisite, the assigned trade supplying the correction and evidence, and the superintendent approving the handoff. The room's status changes with the evidence and approval. The 3D view makes the location clear; the operational value is the decision and work that follows.

The longer-term vision can describe expanding from this repeated workflow into broader construction operations. Keep the distinction between working software, simulated demo behavior, planned capabilities and long-term ambition explicit.

### Where the opportunity could be

Start with the segment already proposed in our pitch: a mid-size US general contractor or developer-builder delivering repeated multifamily units. Pre-drywall readiness is one useful first workflow to test, not an established commitment or a proven market gap.

A focused product could be attractive if crews can participate quickly, PMs immediately understand what remains blocked, and the entire capture-and-review process takes less effort than their current method. We can build toward those advantages without first reproducing procurement, contracts, budgets and every RFI workflow.

The defensible claim today is that we are building around a specific field coordination outcome. We have not yet demonstrated that existing products solve it poorly, that our AI is more accurate, or that buyers will pay an additional subscription.

### Price and proof

The existing pitch proposes $1,000–2,500 per active project/month with unlimited users. That is a pricing hypothesis, not evidence of being cheaper. For perspective, ten Fieldwire paid seats at the listed annual-billing rates total $390–890 per month. The feature coverage, user count and contract scope differ; compare the actual workflow and full customer bill before making a price claim. [Fieldwire pricing](https://www.fieldwire.com/pricing/).

Measure total effort, including crew capture, PM review, setup and support. More complete records are valuable only if the process is practical enough for teams to keep using.

### Next validation

1. Use one real example of an incomplete room, a missed prerequisite or a failed handoff from an accessible US GC. Establish how the team handled it and what it cost in time.
2. Ask OpenSpace and Fieldwire to demonstrate that same sequence. Include missing evidence, conflicting updates and a revised drawing. Compare to the GC's current Procore workflow where applicable.
3. Build and test our complete version of that sequence with real users. Track manual touches, capture/review time, reopened issues and time to accepted closure.
4. Run a limited paid pilot. Record earlier corrections and handoff failures, label estimated avoided costs, and check whether the buyer wants to renew.

**My recommendation:** Continue with the focused product and hackathon demonstration. Pitch the larger operating system as ambition. The current idea is specific enough to test and explain; its advantage should come from making this workflow measurably easier, not from saying nobody else offers construction software or AI.

## Remaining diligence limits

- This is a US-headquarters subset of the original research, not a complete US-market competitor search. It does not establish an absence of smaller local competitors. Autodesk Construction Cloud, Raken, CompanyCam and Buildertrend remain outside this detailed comparison.
- A company's headquarters does not determine where it competes. The report intentionally omits internationally headquartered businesses at the user's request.
- Private-company revenue, retention, runway and paying-customer counts were generally unavailable. Funding is not a substitute for these.
- LinkedIn bands can lag staffing changes. Procore's employee figure is dated, not a current October payroll count.
- OpenSpace's valuation is from 2022; Fieldwire's figure is an acquisition value; Trunk Tools' valuation is Forge's estimate; Procore's is a dated market cap. They are not directly comparable valuation measures.
- Product capabilities and claimed scale need live verification. Absence from a vendor webpage does not demonstrate that a feature is missing.
